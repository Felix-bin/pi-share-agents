# When Nanjing became the Chinese national capital

**The Jin dynasty.** Nanjing is the city where the Yongle Emperor greeted the person the edict was addressed to, and the corpus dates its national-capital status to the Jin dynasty.

The chain, each link grounded in the corpus:

- **The edict's addressee.** `002-Sino-Tibetan-relations-during-the-Ming-dynasty.md:3` speaks of "an edict of the Zhengtong Emperor (r. 1435–1449) addressed to the Karmapa in 1445 … delivered to the Great Treasure Prince of Dharma, the Karmapa." This is the only "edict … addressed to <person>" in the corpus.
- **The person Yongle greeted, and where.** `003-Sino-Tibetan-relations-during-the-Ming-dynasty.md:3`: "The Yongle Emperor came out of the palace in **Nanjing** to greet **the Karmapa** and did not require him to kowtow." `016-…:3` identifies the title the edict uses — "Great Treasure Prince of Dharma" — as the one Yongle bestowed on Deshin Shekpa in Nanjing. `009-…:3` names Deshin Shekpa as the 5th Karmapa Lama whom Yongle invited in 1403. So the greeting city is Nanjing.
- **When that city became the Chinese national capital.** `001-Nanjing.md:3`: "Although as a city located in southern part of China **becoming Chinese national capital as early as in Jin dynasty**, the name Nanjing was designated to the city in Ming dynasty, about a thousand years later." Across the corpus the string "national capital" occurs in this one file only, and the sentence attaches it to the Jin dynasty.

Two reservations, kept rather than resolved: the identification of the 1445 addressee with Yongle's guest rests only on the shared title "Great Treasure Prince of Dharma" (the edict names no personal name, and Deshin Shekpa is dated to 1384–1415 in `009`), so it is an inference, not an explicit statement; and other Nanjing files give differently-anchored capital dates — 1368 (`063`), 1644 (`051`), 1912 (`048`) — none of which uses the phrase "Chinese national capital", which is why the Jin-dynasty wording of `001` is the answer the evidence supports.

**What it rests on:** `001-Nanjing.md:3` (unique phrase "Chinese national capital … as early as in Jin dynasty"), `002-…:3` (edict addressee), `003-…:3` (Yongle greets the Karmapa in Nanjing), corroborated by `016-…:3` and `009-…:3`; corpus-wide greps confirming "national capital" is unique to `001` and the only Yongle–Karmapa greeting is in Nanjing.

**Still open:** whether the intended reference is the un-dated "Jin dynasty" phrasing or one of the dated capital events (1368/1644/1912); and whether the edict addressee and Yongle's guest are the same individual.
