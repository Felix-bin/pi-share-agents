# When did Nanjing become the Chinese national capital?

**By the corpus's own phrasing: as early as in the Jin dynasty.** The single document in the worktree that uses the exact property the question asks for is `001-Nanjing.md`, whose third sentence reads: "Although as a city located in southern part of China **becoming Chinese national capital as early as in Jin dynasty**, the name Nanjing was designated to the city in Ming dynasty, about a thousand years later." The phrase "Chinese national capital" occurs in no other file, and no file gives a numeric year under that label — the source supplies only the dynastic name **Jin dynasty**.

The three-hop chain that identifies the city is confirmed by full reads:

1. **The edict and its addressee.** `002-Sino-Tibetan-relations-during-the-Ming-dynasty.md` (line 3) preserves "an edict of the Zhengtong Emperor (r. 1435–1449) **addressed to the Karmapa** in 1445 … delivered to the Great Treasure Prince of Dharma, the Karmapa." It is the only edict in the corpus phrased as addressed to a person.
2. **The city where Yongle greeted that addressee.** `003-Sino-Tibetan-relations-during-the-Ming-dynasty.md` (line 3): "The Yongle Emperor came out of the palace in **Nanjing** to greet the Karmapa." The addressee's identity is fixed by `016-Sino-Tibetan-relations-during-the-Ming-dynasty.md`, which says Deshin Shekpa was bestowed the title "Great Treasure Prince of Dharma" while in Nanjing — the same title the edict of `002` names. No other file names a city where Yongle greeted a Karmapa.
3. **The capital status of that city.** `001-Nanjing.md` (line 3), quoted above.

So the answer to the question, under the corpus's own wording, is the **Jin dynasty**.

Two things the evidence does not settle, reported rather than resolved:

- **No year is established.** The corpus states a dynasty, not a date, for Nanjing's status as "Chinese national capital"; the only Jin reference in the whole corpus is this sentence in `001`.
- **Other capital statements exist under different wording.** `063-Nanjing.md` (Hongwu "made it the dynastic capital in 1368"), `048-Nanjing.md` (the Republic of China in January 1912 "selected … as its new capital"), and `051-Nanjing.md` (Zhu Yousong enthroned in Nanjing in June 1644). Each uses a different term ("dynastic capital", "new capital", enthronement) and so does not satisfy the question's exact property; which, if any, the question intends beyond the verbatim "Chinese national capital" match cannot be determined from the documents alone.

There is also an internal inconsistency in the sources that touches the chain's hop 1 but not its answer: `002` dates the edict to 1445, while `009` gives Deshin Shekpa's dates as 1384–1415.

**What this rests on:** full reads of `001-Nanjing.md`, `002-Sino-Tibetan-relations-during-the-Ming-dynasty.md`, and `003-Sino-Tibetan-relations-during-the-Ming-dynasty.md`; corroborating read of `016-Sino-Tibetan-relations-during-the-Ming-dynasty.md`; and corpus-wide searches for "greet", "addressed to", "national capital", and "Jin dynasty", each returning the single relevant file cited above.

**Still open:** an explicit year for Nanjing's Chinese-national-capital status; and the intended reading of the three differently-worded competing capital dates.

ANSWER: Jin dynasty
